## Claude / Anthropic design news

- **Time** covers the full scope of the U.S. government's export-control directive: Anthropic received the order at 5:21 pm ET on June 13 and was required to abruptly disable Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for all customers — including foreign national employees — to comply. The directive cites a discovered jailbreak method and cyber-misuse risk. ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/06/13/anthropic-fable-mythos-ban-US-security/))

- Broadband Breakfast notes that Anthropic took the models offline same-day, calling the compliance timeline unusually tight, and flags that the ban's reach (all foreign nationals, even U.S.-resident green card holders) is a first for export controls applied to a consumer AI product. ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/anthropic-said-friday-it-took-its-latest-ai-models-offline-to-comply-with-export-controls/))

- Analysis piece walks through why the directive lands at a particularly sensitive moment: Anthropic confidentially filed for an IPO earlier this month at a ~$965 billion valuation — and restrictions on model availability could rattle investor enthusiasm before the listing. ([ExplainX](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/us-government-bans-fable-5-mythos-5-anthropic-export-control-2026))

## Ethereum

- Ethereum's ERC-8126 AI Agent Verification Standard — which uses zero-knowledge proofs to produce a 0–100 risk score for on-chain AI agents — reached finalized status. The standard defines five verification layers (token, media, Solidity, web app, wallet) and provides a privacy-preserving way to attest agent trustworthiness without revealing underlying data. ([CryptoNews](https://cryptonews.net/news/blockchain/33002602/))

## Stablecoins

- Circle submitted formal comments to the OCC calling for a clear regulatory divide between payment stablecoins and tokenized bank deposits under the GENIUS Act, arguing the two instruments serve fundamentally different purposes and should not share a compliance framework. ([CoinGape](https://coingape.com/genius-act-circle-seeks-clear-divide-between-payment-stablecoins-and-tokenized-deposits/))

- Blockchain.news coverage of Circle's OCC comment letter emphasises that tokenized deposits (like JPMorgan's JPMD) carry on-balance-sheet bank liability risk that payment stablecoins do not, and that conflating them in rulemaking could undermine consumer protection goals. ([Blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/circle-comments-occ-genius-act-stablecoin-rules))
