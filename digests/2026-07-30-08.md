## Solana

- Solana activated SIMD-0286 on mainnet on July 29, raising the per-block compute-unit ceiling by 66% from 60M to 100M CUs — the largest single block-capacity increase in the network's history; no issues were reported at activation and slot times held at 400 ms. ([CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-block-capacity-rises-simd-0286/))
- Analysts are eyeing an $80 SOL retest following the upgrade, while $73.75 remains the critical support level to defend; a sustained close below could expose $60. ([The Coin Republic](https://www.thecoinrepublic.com/2026/07/29/solana-crypto-launches-major-upgrade-on-mainnet-as-bulls-target-80-sol-price-retest/))

## Stablecoins

- The GENIUS Act's mid-July rulemaking deadline came and went without a coordinated final rule package; OCC, FDIC and other agencies released only proposed rules with comment windows running into August, leaving issuers operating under unfinished regulations. ([Crypto Daily](https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/07/genius-act-missed-deadline-stablecoin-uncertainty))
- ABA and three major bank associations sent a letter to Senate leaders warning that a loophole in the GENIUS Act could allow non-bank stablecoin issuers to pay interest/yield, threatening the deposit base that funds community lending. ([ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/07/bankers-local-lending-at-risk-if-stablecoin-payment-of-interest-loophole-isnt-closed/))

## Stablecoins Development in the TradFi Space

- A consortium of major US banks including JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup, HSBC, and Wells Fargo unveiled plans for a shared tokenized deposit network operated by The Clearing House, enabling 24/7 blockchain-based settlements from within the regulated banking perimeter — a direct counterplay to stablecoin issuers. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/banks-unveil-blockchain-network-stablecoin-211954629.html))
- Forbes analysis argues the bank consortium faces a steep historical coordination problem: The Clearing House members have tried and failed to build shared payment infrastructure before, and deposit tokens may encounter the same turf-war dynamics. ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2026/07/28/americas-biggest-banks-are-building-one-deposit-token-history-is-the-hard-part/))
