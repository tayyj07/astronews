## Stablecoins

- The Federal Reserve, FinCEN, OCC, FDIC, and NCUA jointly proposed requiring permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs) to implement bank-style customer identification programs (CIPs) under the GENIUS Act — collecting name, date of birth, address, and government ID before onboarding any customer. The proposal opens a 60-day public comment period ahead of Federal Register publication on June 22. Powell backed the rule; Fed Chair Warsh abstained. ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/treasury-agencies-propose-kyc-rule-for-stablecoin-issuers))
- Ledger Insights covers the joint proposal in depth, noting regulators scoped CIP obligations to direct-to-consumer services only — rejecting a broader "global" KYC requirement as impractical. ([Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/fincen-occ-fed-release-stablecoin-customer-identification-proposals/))
- Crypto Briefing frames the Fed move as pushing stablecoin issuers toward bank-style compliance, with Circle and Paxos well-positioned but smaller issuers facing a steeper climb. ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/fed-proposes-stablecoin-customer-screening-rules/))
- CoinGape focuses on the GENIUS Act implementation angle: the proposal marks one of the first binding rulemaking steps since the Act's enactment in July 2025. ([CoinGape](https://coingape.com/fed-proposes-customer-id-rules-for-stablecoin-issuers-under-genius-act-framework/))

The GENIUS Act's implementation phase is accelerating across multiple axes simultaneously: the Fed-led multi-agency KYC rulemaking, TradFi payment giants absorbing stablecoin rails (Nuvei/Payoneer), and infrastructure funding rounds (Trace Finance) all landed within the same 6-hour window — signalling that the post-legislation buildout is now moving from policy to execution.
