## Stablecoins

- Bank of England publishes its final draft regulatory framework for systemic stablecoins: a temporary £40 billion ($52.8B) per-coin issuance cap replaces earlier plans to restrict individual retail (£20K) and business (£10M) holdings; the permitted share of UK government bond backing is raised to 70% from 60%; consultation closes 22 September 2026 with a final Code of Practice targeted by end-2026, paving the way for regulated stablecoins to operate in the UK from 2027. ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2026/june/boe-launches-policy-statement-and-draft-rules-on-regulating-systemic-stablecoins))
- BoE Deputy Governor Breeden signalled the central bank "wants UK stablecoins to work," with the eased framework seen as a direct response to industry warnings that the original stricter caps risked ceding the UK's competitive position in digital assets. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-22/boe-sets-40-billion-cap-per-stablecoin-and-drops-holding-limit))

## Stablecoins Development in the TradFi Space

- The BoE's softer stance — replacing per-holder caps with a systemic £40B issuance limit — is being read by analysts as a pivot toward enabling sterling stablecoins to compete with tokenised deposits in wholesale and retail payments; the framework explicitly carves out non-systemic crypto-trading uses, leaving those under FCA supervision only. ([Global Banking & Finance](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/bank-england-softens-stablecoin-rules-final-framework/))

## Cross-cutting Themes

The Bank of England's June 22 policy statement is the single major development of this window, with stablecoin regulation and TradFi adoption converging: the UK joins the US GENIUS Act and EU MiCA as the third major jurisdiction to publish a firm stablecoin rulebook in 2026.
