## Stablecoins

- Federal Reserve researchers proposed that payment stablecoins could qualify for M1 (everyday payments use) or M2 (short-term value store), but flagged a critical double-counting problem: reserve dollars backing stablecoins already sit inside the existing monetary aggregates, which would inflate the measured money supply. The authors stressed the paper reflects personal views, not Fed policy. ([Crypto.news](https://crypto.news/stablecoins-could-enter-m1-or-m2-fed-study-says/)) ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/fed-economists-stablecoin-research-exposes-how-the-same-dollar-could-count-twice-in-m1-or-m2/))

## Stablecoins Development in TradFi

- The Fed M1/M2 research note has direct TradFi implications: if USD-pegged stablecoins are eventually classified inside U.S. monetary aggregates, banks may face new reserve-requirement, deposit-insurance, and capital-treatment questions for the stablecoin liabilities they plan to issue — a live concern for the 21-bank consortium targeting a 2027 stablecoin launch. ([COINOTAG](https://en.coinotag.com/fed-note-usdc-money-supply-double-counting))

## Cross-cutting themes

**Stablecoin monetary-policy integration:** The Fed M1/M2 paper is the most consequential policy-adjacent signal of the week — it opens the question of whether stablecoins will sit inside or beside the traditional money supply, with knock-on effects for reserve requirements, the GENIUS Act rulemaking, and the 21-bank consortium's planned USD stablecoin.
